About

Research Background

Ali Hasan is a Marie Sklodowska-Curie Actions doctoral researcher in the TOPOCOM network, affiliated with the University of Messina (Italy) in the PETASPIN group under Prof. Giovanni Finocchio. His doctoral research centers on building GPU-accelerated numerical solvers for condensed matter physics, implemented in CUDA C++ using the Thrust parallel programming library.

The main output of his doctoral work is PETASPIN_microelectrics, a GPU-based solver for ferroelectric materials grounded in the Time-Dependent Ginzburg-Landau (TDGL) and Landau-Khalatnikov-Tani (LKT) equations. By exploiting the massively parallel architecture of modern GPUs, the solver achieves speedups exceeding 100 times over equivalent CPU codes for large simulation domains. The software includes a Python-based graphical interface requiring no programming knowledge from the end user, and is publicly available through the TOPOCOM consortium website accompanied by a preprint submitted to Physical Review Applied.

A significant part of his work involves extending the PetaSpin GPU micromagnetic solver with the Landau-Lifshitz-Bloch (LLB) equation for finite-temperature micromagnetic simulations, introducing temperature-dependent exchange stiffness, anisotropy, and damping constants following Callen-Callen scaling theory. This work also includes a GPU-based study of ferrons, quasi-particle excitations in ferroelectrics analogous to magnons in ferromagnets, with applications in domain wall-based computing and switching.

In parallel, Ali contributed micromagnetic simulations with the PetaSpin solver to support a machine learning approach for estimating the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya Interaction (DMI) from experimental skyrmion bubble data, resulting in a co-authored preprint submitted to Applied Physics Letters.

Publications & Preprints

Two preprints are available on arXiv. Additional papers are in preparation covering ferron physics and LLB-based domain wall dynamics.

Preprint on arXiv · First Author · Physical Review Applied (under review)
A GPU-based Solver for Polarization Dynamics in Ferroelectric Materials
Ali Hasan is first author. This paper presents PETASPIN_microelectrics: a CUDA C++ solver implementing the TDGL and LKT equations with GPU parallelism, validated against BaTiO3 phase transitions, domain wall profiles, and hysteresis loops. Speedups exceeding 100 times over CPU are demonstrated for large simulation domains.

Preprint on arXiv · Co-Author · Applied Physics Letters (under review)
Data-Driven Estimation of the Interfacial Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya Interaction with Machine Learning
Ali Hasan contributed GPU-based micromagnetic simulations using the PetaSpin LLG solver, generating over 1,400 simulation datasets across a wide range of external field conditions to stabilize skyrmion bubbles. A neural network trained on this dataset is proposed as a machine-learning method to estimate DMI strength from experimental data.
arXiv:2603.28812
Manuscript in preparation
Ferrons in Ferroelectric Materials: Quasi-Particle Excitations and Their Dispersion Relations
Draft under active development. This work investigates ferrons as the ferroelectric analog of magnons, derives their dispersion relations from first principles, and explores their application potential in domain wall-based computing and energy-efficient switching in perovskite ferroelectrics.

Current-Induced Temperature-Dependent Domain Wall Dynamics Using the LLB Model
Draft under active development. This paper applies the GPU-accelerated LLB framework to study how current-driven forces alter domain wall motion at finite temperature, with implications for thermally assisted magnetic switching.

Software

GPU-accelerated simulation tools developed during the PhD, publicly available through the TOPOCOM consortium.

PETASPIN_microelectrics
Lead Developer
A GPU-accelerated phase-field solver for ferroelectric materials implementing the TDGL and LKT equations. Written in CUDA C++ with the Thrust parallel library, it delivers speedups exceeding 100 times over CPU implementations. The solver has been validated against BaTiO3 phase transitions, domain wall profiles, and PbTiO3 hysteresis loops. It ships with a Python and PyQt5 graphical interface requiring no programming experience, supports multiple parallel simulations, and is publicly released through the TOPOCOM consortium website.

PetaSpin LLB Extension
Developer (in progress)
An extension of the PetaSpin GPU micromagnetic solver incorporating the Landau-Lifshitz-Bloch equation for finite-temperature micromagnetic simulations. The implementation applies temperature-dependent exchange stiffness and anisotropy constants following Callen-Callen theory, corrects the exchange Laplacian for LLB longitudinal dynamics, and provides a hard-plane anisotropy formulation that eliminates spurious magnetization drift. Enables domain wall width benchmarking against analytical predictions across a wide temperature range.
